# CPG-EI compositional out-of-grammar benchmark v1

## Research question

Does a trainable relational learner recover compositional Boolean structure when the target functions are outside the finite single-operator grammar used by the symbolic discovery baseline?

## Out-of-grammar construction

Each output is generated by:

`(x_a AND x_b) XOR (x_c AND x_d)`

where the four input indices are distinct and selected independently per teacher output. The symbolic baseline searches single declared operators (XOR, AND, OR, NAND, threshold, ordered implication, and conditional XOR); it does not contain XOR-of-conjunctions as a compositional rule.

Default protocol: 6 Boolean inputs, 4 outputs, 8 independent teacher families per seed, 3 seeds, and all 64 states per family. Each family has disjoint 32/16/16 train/development/locked state partitions.

## Compared methods

1. Finite symbolic discovery: minimum training error, development tie-break, locked report-only.
2. Full polynomial learner: complete Boolean monomial basis with development-loss early stopping.
3. Symbolic-support hybrid: training-only finite-rule support selection, monomial expansion, and development-loss early stopping.

All methods receive the same corpus and state partitions. Each family is fitted independently.

## Leakage and reproducibility controls

- Deterministic seed-specific teacher functions and state partitions.
- Corpus SHA-256 in every seed report.
- Tests mutate development/locked labels and verify that selected symbolic rules and hybrid support features do not change.
- Locked labels are never used for candidate selection, feature selection, or checkpoint selection.
- Report mean, sample standard deviation, minimum and maximum across seeds.

## Interpretation boundary

This is **operator-grammar OOD**, not held-out-family zero-shot transfer. Every model receives labeled training examples from every teacher family. It is also not a natural-language, causal, Yi-Xue, or real-world reasoning benchmark.

The in-grammar PR #422 benchmark showed 99.90% locked bit accuracy for exhaustive symbolic search in 11.54 seconds, 86.13% for the full polynomial learner in 1,467.69 seconds, and 90.04% for the symbolic-support hybrid in 711.25 seconds. Those results motivate testing whether learning becomes useful outside the symbolic grammar; they do not predict the OOD outcome.

No architecture is declared superior in advance. A learning method earns value here only if it improves locked performance on the compositional targets at a defensible compute cost.
